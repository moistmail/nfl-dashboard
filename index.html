How to read this page
  • Model numbers come only from our power ratings. Market numbers are what the sportsbook line implies. They are shown side by side so you can see where they differ.
  • The large score is the model's projected score. The bar under the numbers is the away team's model win chance.
  • Spread such as “CHI -2.5” means Chicago is expected to win by about 2.5 points. Total is both teams’ points combined. Win probability is the favorite’s chance of winning outright.
  • Gap tags show how far the model is from the market. A gap of 3 or more points is highlighted. Backtests show the market is usually right, so treat those as research leads, not bets.
  • Spread picks and over/under picks are graded separately on the Model performance page.

Glossary

Plain-English definitions for the terms used on this page.

Betting basics

Spread
The number of points the favorite is expected to win by. “CHI -2.5” means Chicago is favored by 2.5; the other side is “+2.5”.
Favorite / underdog
The team expected to win, and the team expected to lose.
Pick’em
A game with no favorite: the spread is zero.
Cover
Beating the spread. A 3-point favorite covers by winning by 4 or more; the underdog covers by losing by 2 or fewer, or winning.
Push
A bet that lands exactly on the line, so the stake is returned.
Total (over/under)
The combined points both teams are expected to score. You bet whether the real total goes over or under it.
Moneyline
A bet on who wins outright, with no spread. -150 means risk $150 to win $100; +130 means risk $100 to win $130.
Vig (juice)
The sportsbook’s cut, built into the odds. At the standard -110 you risk $110 to win $100.
Break-even (52.4%)
How often you must win at -110 odds just to break even. Anything below it loses money over time.
Closing line
The final line just before kickoff. It reflects the most information, so it is the hardest number to beat.
Closing-line value (CLV)
Getting a better number than the closing line, such as +3.5 on a team that closes +2.5. Beating the close consistently is the best early sign of real skill.

What the model produces

Power rating
How many points better or worse a team is than an average team on a neutral field. +4.0 means four points better than average.
Off rating
Points a team scores above or below the league average per game. Higher is better.
Def rating
Points a team allows versus the league average. Lower is better: -3.0 means it allows three fewer than average.
Projected score
The model's projected total split between the two teams by its projected margin.
Win chance
The probability the favorite wins outright.
Market
The sportsbooks’ line and total for the game.
Gap
How far the model is from the market, in points. “Model likes CHI by 4.8” means the ratings rate Chicago 4.8 points better than the market does.
Highlighted gap
A gap of 3 or more points, shown in orange. It usually means the market knows something the ratings do not, such as an injury, so check before acting.

Model inputs

Home field advantage
Points added for playing at home. Not applied at neutral sites.
Last-season carryover
How much of each team’s final rating from last season it starts this season with. 70% means it keeps most of it.
Shrink toward prior
How many games of this season it takes for current results to count as much as the starting rating. A higher number means ratings move more slowly.
Strength of schedule
Adjusts ratings for the quality of opponents faced, so beating strong teams counts for more.
Margin std dev
How much real results scatter around a projection, in points. It converts an expected margin into a win chance.

Results and testing

Spread record (ATS)
How often the model's side beat the closing spread. If the model rates the home team higher than the market does, its pick is the home team to cover; otherwise the away team. Pushes are shown as a third number.
Totals record (over/under)
How often the model's over or under call was right against the closing total. The model picks the over when its projected total is above the market total, and the under when it is below.
3+ pt gaps
The same records, counting only games where the model differed from the market line by 3 or more points.
Winner pick
Whether the team with the higher model win probability won outright. The market's favorite is graded the same way for comparison.
Avg miss
The average gap, in points, between a projection and what happened. Lower is better. Also called mean absolute error.
Point-in-time
Each game is predicted using only games played before its week, so past results are a fair test.
Backtest
Running the model over past seasons to see how its projections would have done.
Brier score
A score for win probabilities: the average squared gap between the stated chance and the outcome. Lower is better; 0.25 is a coin flip.
